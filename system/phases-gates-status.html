Status, Phases & Decision Gates

Three things that sound alike and get constantly confused. They answer three different questions, live in three different places, and roll up differently. This is the line between them.

Home → System → Status, Phases & Decision Gates
The Big Picture
Three axes, never one

Every project carries three independent attributes. They are not versions of the same thing — they are three different questions, and each gets its own field. The moment one field tries to be two of these, the system drifts. That drift is exactly what this article exists to prevent.

The one-line test Status = where it is. Gate = may we proceed. Phase = how the work is grouped. If a field can't answer exactly one of those, it's on the wrong axis.
Axis 1
Status — the business situation

Status (also called the project's Situation) is the coarse lifecycle bucket — the single answer to “where is this in the business?” A project is in exactly one situation at a time, and it's how leadership scans the whole portfolio at a glance.

  • Biz Dev — sourcing and qualifying opportunities; not yet under contract. (Has its own pipeline sub-stages — e.g. Warm 6–12 mo, Hot 0–6 mo.)
  • Pipeline — under contract / in diligence and capitalization; working toward a close.
  • WIP — closed and in execution (pre-con through construction).
  • Closeout & Warranty — substantially complete; punch, final accounting, warranty period.
  • Asset Management — stabilized and held / operated as an ongoing asset.
  • Closed — sold or otherwise fully wound down.

Status rolls up to the division and company — a company's portfolio is simply its projects grouped by situation (“how many are in WIP, how many still in Pipeline”).

Axis 2
Decision Gates — the investment ladder

A decision gate is a go / no-go on committing the next round of capital. It is not how the work is grouped — it is whether we're allowed to spend the next dollar. Gates run in a fixed ladder; you don't skip one. The canonical source is the Decision Gate Task Library (81 tasks across 8 gates).

GateNameWhat clearing it authorizes
G0Initial QualificationKill early — or authorize pursuit of an LOI.
G1LOI & Contract ExecutionExecute the PSA with a DD period. First hard-money exposure.
G2aGo Hard on DepositDeposit goes non-refundable. Diligence complete.
G2bClose AuthorizationCapital stack confirmed. Authorize the close.
G3Pre-Construction ApprovalFull pre-con budget approved; permit-ready CDs.
G4Construction Start (NTP)Full capital deployed; notice to proceed.
G5Mid-Construction Checkpoint~50% complete — recast or continue.
—Property Listing (Brokerage)Enter the listing contract; marketing goes live.

Each gate holds two kinds of items:

  • Rating tasks — the decision criteria, scored 0 / 1 / 2 by the responsible functional lead across five elements: Strategic Fit, Market / Product Fit, Financial Viability, Constructability, Jurisdictional & Legal (plus Operational Capacity). The scored result is the go / no-go.
  • Pre-gate tasks — execution checklist items (Pre-Pursuit, Pre-DD, Pre-Close, Pre-Con…) that must be done before the gate can be called.
Who owns a gate Departments own a gate window — e.g., Estimating owns G0–G3, then hands off. A gate is a checkpoint on the investment timeline, not a container for work.
Axis 3
Phases — the work breakdown

A phase is a chunk of the project's work — and it carries both time and money. The “Due Diligence” phase has DD tasks (schedule) and DD costs (budget). That is the entire point: a phase is the shared grouping across the schedule and the budget, so “Due Diligence” is one thing — not two strings that happen to match.

Phases are defined in the budgeting ecosystem (that's the source of truth), and the identical phase set extends to tasks and the schedule. A phase can carry competing phase options (scenarios) — two ways to do Site Work, priced side by side — with one selected as active.

Phases are project-specific: a ground-up development and a disposition don't share a phase list. And they are not the fixed gate ladder — a phase is how work is grouped, not a checkpoint.

How They Fit
Interlocked — but never merged
  • A gate authorizes entering the next stretch of phases. G3/G4 authorize the construction phases — you don't start the Vertical phase before G4.
  • Clearing a gate often flips the status. Passing G2b (Close) moves a project from Pipeline into WIP.
  • A phase never authorizes anything, and a gate never holds a budget. A budget line filed under “G3” is the creep — G3 is a gate; the money belongs to a phase.
Most common mistake Using a gate code (G3) as a phase tag, or a phase name as a status. One axis per field. Always.
The Lifecycle
How a phase moves: underwriting → gate → execution

A phase is one identity that matures across the deal. It is not re-created at each stage — the same phase you underwrite is the one project management executes.

1

Underwriting — decide what to build

Each phase holds competing options (scope A vs B; each option carries its own cost and timeline). A scenario picks one option per phase and drives the numbers.

2

Decision gate — go / no-go

A checkpoint on the next round of capital. Clearing it locks the picks and authorizes entering the next stretch of phases.

3

Execution (PM) — run the work

The picked option becomes the real work: the phase now carries tasks (schedule) + committed budget, and the project manager executes it.

Options vs base / best / worst Options are discrete competing scopes you pick between — each its own cost + timeline. Base / best / worst is a separate sensitivity lens on the plan you already picked — same scope, flex the assumptions. Different tools; don't conflate them.

Because it's one phase throughout, a task can carry both a gate and a phase: its phase is the work it advances (Site Work); its gate is the checkpoint it serves (G4). Example: a CrossMod diligence task sits under the Acquisition & DD phase, tagged gate G0 — gate and phase, side by side, never merged.

Structure
How it rolls up

task → phase → project → master project / division / company.

  • Tasks roll up to their phase (contributing both time and money).
  • Phases roll up to the project (its full schedule and budget).
  • Projects roll up to a master project, a division, and/or a company.
  • Status and gate roll up to portfolio dashboards — “how many projects are in WIP,” “how many are stuck before G2b.”
The Rule
Reuse, don't creep

One phase list per project, owned by the budget and shared by the schedule and tasks. One gate catalog — the Decision Gate Task Library. One status field — the six situations. If a fourth place starts storing any of these, that is drift — consolidate it back to the source. Same discipline everywhere: reuse the existing structure before creating a new one.
