# The Game System — Situations, Phases, Pillars & the Score
### Platform Fundamentals · Course 2

*This is the mental model the whole platform runs on. Read it once, slowly. Everything else — projects, assets, divisions, dashboards, Kompass — is an expression of what's here.*

---

## 1. Why a "game"?

Walk into a room where a game is on TV and you instantly ask three questions:

> **Who's playing? · What's the score? · How much time is left?**

Those three answers give you *situational awareness* — you know what matters and you can engage. Most work fails to feel like winning because those questions can't be answered: we drown in tasks and meetings and lose the thread of progress. The platform exists to keep those three questions answerable for **every game we play** — a construction project, a stabilized asset, a division, or the whole company.

A well-run game also answers seven alignment questions: **where are we going, why, when, where are we now, how far left, how much time, and who owns what.** If a team can't answer those, it's planning to fail.

---

## 2. The two kinds of situations (this is the key idea)

A **situation** is "the chunk you're winning right now." Situations come in **two modes**, and confusing them is the single most common way people get lost.

**Lifecycle situations — sequential.** Work that is *moving toward completion* passes through ordered stages with clean handoffs. A construction project moves **Business Development → Pipeline → Work in Progress → Closeout & Customer Service.** You're in exactly one at a time, and you graduate to the next.

**Ongoing situations — parallel.** Work that is *being run, not finished* holds several situations at once, forever. A stabilized asset runs **Revenue, Retention, Maintenance, Financial, Capital Markets, Investor Reporting, and Risk** simultaneously — none of them "completes," they're all tended continuously.

> A dev project **moves through** situations. A finished asset **holds a scoreboard of** situations. Same word, two motions.

This is why a division (HR, Accounting, Marketing) feels different from a construction job — a division is mostly an *ongoing* game.

---

## 3. The two axes of every game (this resolves the confusion)

Every game is scored on **two independent axes**. Score only one and you lose in a predictable way.

| Axis | The question | Mechanism | Cadence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Progress** | "Where are we — what's the next unlock?" | **Phases + Gates** — *achieve X to unlock Y* | sequential |
| **Health** | "Are the fundamentals sound?" | **Pillars** — always tended | continuous |

**Progress = Phases and Gates.** A *phase* is a stage; a *gate* is the qualification between phases ("you must have permitted drawings before you start building"). Gates standardize decisions and give work **priority and focus**. *Neglect this axis and you get a pile of projects floating aimlessly, none of them clearly next.*

**Health = Pillars.** A *pillar* is a dimension you must **always tend**, regardless of phase. On a construction project the pillars are **Alignment, Budget, Schedule, QC & Safety.** *Neglect this axis and you hit every progress gate while something quietly breaks — like a runner hitting every training milestone but not sleeping or eating. The scoreboard looks fine right up until it doesn't.*

**A game is only truly green when both axes are green.** Progress without health is fragile; health without progress is aimless. The platform surfaces **two GYRs** — a *Progress* GYR (on pace to the next gate?) and a *Health* GYR (pillars tended?) — so you always see both.

---

## 4. It's fractal — the same two axes at every level

Phases/Gates and Pillars aren't just a project thing. **Every game — project, asset, division, company — has both axes.**

| Level | **Progress** (Phases + Gates) | **Health** (Pillars / ongoing situations, tended) |
|---|---|---|
| **Dev project** | G0–G5 dev gates (BD → Pipeline → WIP → Closeout) | Alignment · Budget · Schedule · QC & Safety |
| **Finished asset** | *(largely complete — stabilized)* | the 7 ongoing situations (Revenue, Retention, …) |
| **Division / product line** | maturation phases: *Blueprint + KB built → gate → operator picks up the flag → gate → scale* | that division's ongoing situations |
| **Company** | strategic build phases | company-level health areas |

**Worked example — Entry-Level Housing (a product line, i.e. an ongoing game):** its *build* has phases — you must have a **project blueprint + knowledge base** before you can hand the flag to someone to run projects while they learn (Phase 1 → gate → Phase 2 → scale). Meanwhile its *health* (pipeline volume, project margins, operator competency) is tended continuously. Two axes, one level up from a single project.

---

## 5. The ladder (where everything lives)

| Tier | What it is | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| **Game / Goal** | What you're playing — a measurable outcome or a standing operating game | 6–18 mo, or perpetual |
| **Key Situation** | The chunk you're winning — a **phase** (lifecycle) or an **ongoing situation** (operating) | varies |
| **Project** | The vehicle that does the work; rolls up to a situation | weeks–months |
| **Phase / Sprint · Task** | Execution inside a project (milestones live here as notable tasks) | days–weeks |

Situations are the bridge between **strategy** ("on the business") and **execution** ("in the business"): *projects roll up to situations, and a situation's score aggregates from its projects and readings.*

---

## 6. The score — how GYR is actually computed

The score is not a feeling; it's a **reading**. Every measurable (a *KPI*) carries **one number**, and its current value comes from a **routine reading** (a "stat"):

- **Feed-first:** where an automated feed exists, readings arrive on a cadence — occupancy from operating statements, weight from a Siri shortcut, activity from Strava, cash from the bank.
- **Routine capture where no feed exists:** the system schedules a prompt so the number gets entered on a rhythm. A game with no readings can't show a score.

Each KPI's **GYR** compares its latest reading to its target/threshold:

> **Super Green (ahead) · Green (on track) · Yellow (at risk) · Red (critical)**

Status **rolls up** — KPI → situation → asset/division/company — as a **default signal that a human can override.** A red KPI *flags attention* but doesn't automatically drag its whole situation red; judgment stays with the owner (a milestone can be red while the goal is still on track).

---

## 7. Commitment — targets are promises

Once a **target** or **due date** is set, it can't be changed casually. Changing one requires a **written reason**, is **logged as a decision**, and takes **elevated permission**. This is deliberate: the integrity of the score depends on not moving the goalposts when things get hard. (Lender covenants get the strongest form of this lock.)

---

## 8. Same engine, your words (vernacular adapts to context)

The platform runs on one set of shared structures, but **the labels you see adapt to the entity or division you're working in.** A construction team sees "gates" and "pillars"; an asset manager sees "situations" and "covenants"; a division lead sees "maturation phases." Under the hood it's the same goals, situations, stats, and roll-ups — the vocabulary meets you where you work, so the words always feel native to the game you're playing.

**And pillars aren't forced where they don't fit.** A stabilized asset isn't scored on Alignment/Budget/Schedule/QC — those are a *project* construct. Instead, the relevant pillars show up only on the **recurring projects** where they apply: an *insurance-renewal* recurring project is scored on **schedule integrity** (renew before expiry), **checklist integrity** (the right coverages are in place), and **budget integrity** (premium within plan). The asset's ongoing *situations* self-score on their own KPIs.

---

## 9. Putting it together — three quick reads

- **A construction project:** lives in *lifecycle mode*. Situation = its current phase; gates push it forward; you tend the 4 pillars the whole way. Two GYRs: on pace to the next gate, and are the fundamentals sound.
- **Cal Ave (a stabilized asset):** lives in *ongoing mode*. No dev gates — it holds 7 parallel situations, each self-scoring on stat-fed KPIs, rolling up to one asset scoreboard. Its recurring projects (budget season, insurance renewal, investor reporting) carry only the pillars that matter to them.
- **A division (HR, Accounting):** an ongoing game with a *maturation phase track* (standing up its system) plus continuous health situations once it's running.

**The move:** whatever you're looking at, ask — *what situation am I winning right now, what's the next gate, and which pillar is slipping?* That's situational awareness, and it's the whole point.
